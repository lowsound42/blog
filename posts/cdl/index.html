Cognitive Death Loop

In which my brain hits itself in the face
Written July 29, 2026 · Modified July 29, 2026 · By Omar
The Thing

Geetha and I have been trying to have a kid for a few years now and it’s just not happening for us. Our journey started out very exciting when we actually began trying; probably like it does for anyone else who decides to grow their family. Then there was this period where it did start to feel a bit weird that things were taking so long. Geetha clocked in much quicker than I did. I naively believed that we’d be those people for whom it took a few months and we’d laugh about it soon enough. At the six month mark we went to see our doctor to see if there was something more than just timing or chance involved and she referred us to a fertility clinic. We’ve been seeing them for about two and a half years now.

Our fertility doctor is great. He’s a lovely guy with a thousand degrees. He’s very clear about what we can try, what we should test, and he’s very kind to us. We’ve been doing IVF and so far we’ve had some failed transfers, one of which was kind of traumatic because it ended up being an ectopic pregnancy. We had to terminate it and then monitor Geetha till her hormone levels indicated she was in the clear.

Part of why it was so traumatic was that it was the first time in all these years we’d ever seen a positive pregnancy test, so we got carried away as we went through it. It was actually a really wonderful few weeks, the clinic called with the test and the nurse excitedly told Geetha that she was pregnant. We called the daycare down the street and put ourselves on the waitlist. And then we started imagining the next few months of our lives; like how we’d introduce Gobi (our dog) to this new person, how we’d stay in our one bedroom condo for as long as we could before we’d have to move, and of course how we’d finally break the news to our families.

That’s part of the thing with infertility, it robs you of the things that people think are normal about having a child. Like being excited about a pregnancy test, planning for the things that people plan for when a child is arriving, or telling the people you care about this wonderful news.

Some time last year, I can’t remember when exactly, we began to talk about how we’d get through this regardless of how things turned out, even if the IVF and all of that just didn’t work for us. And I know that we’ll be okay, I mean the two of us together. It would be hard, but I’ve learned that together we can get through some really difficult life stuff.

So, this not working is a real possibility and we’d have to be prepared for it. One thing that came up was adoption. Should we consider adoption if we decide that our IVF journey is over? We started looking into it and learned a lot about how it works here. There are both public and private adoption processes run by the province and by accredited agencies. And then there’s international adoption that is facilitated by provincially approved consultants. I was drawn to the international adoption from India because that’s where my family is from, but these are all options we’d be open to. There is one catch with the Indian adoption route though. The Central Adoption Resource Authority (CARA) requires a couple to be married for at least 2 years before they are considered eligble to adopt. So we got married in January of this year in a small ceremony at home to get the clock running on that! Now if we do need to go this way, we’ll be totally ready :)

Everything we learned was telling us that if we wanted to pursue adoption we’d need to do so seriously and not engage in other fertility treatment. We decided to continue with our clinic till we were actually mentally done with it, but we knew that these options existed for us to explore if that’s where we ended up.

This has been our lives for almost 3 years now, and because I was thinking about it so much, it changed how I thought about parenthood and parents.

The Other Thing

My father and I do not talk. Like, at all. We’re both angry people and that’s part of it. But there’s more to it than that, I have my reasons and I’ve said them to him. Many times. Repeatedly. Abba has always responded to me with two things.

“I’m the father”


“I can’t change”
 

The first is an appeal to a very simplistic view of family, which is that as the patriarch, he just gets to decide everything, all the time, forever.

The “I can’t change thing” is actually “I won’t change”, but you know, it’s way easier to say can’t.

He knows we’re dealing with this right now but has never really asked us about it or offered any words of encouragement. Which makes sense because like I said, we don’t talk. However he also doesn’t like it when my mother wants to talk to me without him and so my relationship with her has also been negatively affected by our inability to communicate.

In parallel to this paternal angst we have Geetha’s mother who has herself said that her relationship with Geetha has been antagonistic ever since she was a young girl (which to me is kind of wild, like who gets into a feud with a child, but okay).

We’d been engaging in our fertility treatments for a couple of years without saying anything before we told her about it. When we went to their place, she definitely was upset for us, but then went into damage control mode. She told us about how common infertility was, how many people end up not having kids and they’re totally happy, how we should have seen a doctor earlier, etc. Fine things to say about someone in their absence I think, (and maybe true, maybe not) but I think all Geetha needed in that moment was someone to ask how she was feeling and to maybe hug her.

This is how she likes to help though. By questioning every decision we’ve made and by “reassuring us” that we’ll be fine like her friends who don’t have kids.

I know Geetha and I will be fine, we’ll deal with that but we’re in the thick of it right now and it’s not helpful to hear that.

Anyway, after a few months of this Geetha and I decided to tell her how we felt about this and to start a conversation about how we would prefer to be supported. It did not go well. Her parting words to us were

“I won’t be talking to you people anymore”


“It’s awful being the parent to an adult child”
 

She has not spoken a word to us since then.

Brain punching itself

🤜🧠🤛

So here’s the cognitive death loop I’ve put myself in. After being through this infertility journey, if Geetha and I do end up having a kid, whether through IVF or adoption, these are things I could never see myself doing:

  • Feuding with my child while they are still just a child.
  • Refusing to engage with them as an adult simply because we disagree.
  • Hiding behind “I can’t change” when my behavior is causing them distress.
  • Letting months of silence pass without doing everything in my power to start a conversation again.

I know that parenting must be harder that I can imagine, I’m not there yet. And I don’t mean to suggest that I’d be the perfect father. But I do know this: I would never let my pride undercut my relationship with my child.

That’s really what I can’t get past. I mean, I’m sure they could all justify their behaviour their own way, there’s always their side of the story. But if he’s “the father” then I am “the child”, and I could never imagine treating my child that way.
