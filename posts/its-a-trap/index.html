Lessons Learned: None

Round and round we go
Written November 10, 2026 · Modified November 10, 2026 · By Omar

I found a picture of all of us. Of you, of me, of everybody in society. Here, take a look.

The first time around#

Remember when social media didn’t feel like the addictive drug it feels like today? It promised to connect us to the people in our communities and to open the doors to connecting with people in disparate places that once-upon-a-time seemed so inaccessible.

There was a genuinely lovely feeling associated with logging on or in to whatever platform and seeing what was going on. Getting nudged, poked, or seeing an alert pop up weren’t the exhausting mental hurdles they seem to be today.

Facebook kind of made sense at the time. My friends posted pictures of the things they wanted to share, the stuff we did together, the stuff I missed out on. It was a place where groups were formed and events were planned. That was kind of it, and it was enough. Or wait, they added games too at some point. Okay, that’s fine I guess; some of the games are literally just online gambling which is a bit weird but okay. Oh, also it started showing us stuff from people we didn’t know at all and weren’t connected to. Hmm, I don’t get it, but maybe some people made new friends?

As it got bigger, something else started to happen; it became a source for news and information in a way we never really imagined in the early days. If someone had told me when I first got on there that this is where a significant number of people would get their news one day, I legitimately would not have understood what you meant. The place that my friend posted the pictures he took of us smoking cigarettes outside of school that we had to comment on frantically because he had made them public so everyone could see them and that meant that someone might tell my parents PLEASE TAKE THIS OFF OR MAKE IT PRIVATE YOU FUCKER!?!

That place?

Well, apparently 40% of Americans get their news from Facebook and YouTube, check it out. (I don’t mean to pick on Americans, I’m sure this is representative of a majority of the world. It’s just the data I found after a quick search).

Okay, so people are getting their news and information from Facebook, surely this company will build systems to manage this in a responsible way? Right?

OH FUCKING HELL. Crimes against humanity? Ethnic cleansing? BY THE FUCKING PLATFORM THAT LET YOU POKE YOUR FRIENDS?

Okay, we all get it now#

Okay, yeah social media went off the rails. The companies that did built these things started building power and in the relentless pursuit of mARkEt SHaRe, they acquired smaller social media firms just as those companies got interesting and fun. The more users on your infernal poke machine, the more eyeballs you could guarantee on an ad; and so advertising became the entire thing. Acquiring more firms became the easiest way to get more users into your ’ecosystem.’ And we the people, the people who just wanted to go to parties and share our holiday photos? We became the product. The advertisers and the social media giants traded us like whatever it is that kids trade these days.

The conspiracy theories, the calls to violence, the naked racism, the hatred, all of those became features. That’s the stuff that keeps people coming back to the platform.

These stupid companies have become behemoths and they think they can do everything because they made a website where you and your friends can write messages to each other. For the most part people are awake to this, and we’re aware of the extreme damage that social media has done to us as individuals and to our societies. We’ve given these firms far too much power, and we’ve let our brains deteriorate by the power of doomscroll.

This will never happen again!

I’ll fucking do it again#

If you’ve read my blog before then you knew it was just a matter of time before I turned my rambling towards the looming spectre of so called AI.

It’s happening again; in different ways and also in the same ways. New corporate behemoths (and some of the same old ones) have risen to offer us a new toy. This toy is going to change everything!

Sometimes they say it will bring about abundance! (THE AUTOMATIC DICTIONARY MACHINE WILL BRING ABOUT ABUNDANCE EVERYBODY)

Other times they tell us it will destroy everybody! (THE AUTOMATIC DICTIONARY MACHINE WILL DESTROY US ALL)

The words don’t matter; everything they say, they say to get us to use the machine. This time I’m approaching it with the perspective of someone who has been through one civilizational paradigm shift already, and I’m not going in with the naive optimism I had when I was 17.

First off, I will acknowledge that the LLM’s are good at stuff. They are great at coding, that’s my expertise so I’ll stop there. I’ve heard they can be useful for maths (though there is debate around whether we are losing track of what makes maths research useful). There, I said it, it can be useful.

With respect to so called AI in art, music, literature, and the things that make life wonderful, this is just fucking stupid in my opinion. Even if they are “good” at these things, the point of art, the whole fucking point of any art is not just to produce an artifact. The universe flows through the experiences of an individual human being. That person interprets this experience and does…something with it. It comes out as music, as a painting, as a book, as a recipe, as a new football trick. The point of art is to experience, interpret, present to others, and for them to ingest and re-interpret. The LLM is not a conscious human, whatever it does is irrelevant in this field.

Anyway, back to the point. We are once again hurtling head-first to a world in which a handful of corporations are very openly trying to control us by controlling the means of production very much like the social media companies did. Except it is much worse this time. When mass produced LLM bullshit meets social media echo chambers, we are at levels of fucked that we’ve never seen before. Of course, it’s happening already.

Also, and perhaps more insidious, the fake claims that these so called AI CEO guys are making about abundance and changing work point towards their true intentions. They want to sell a product that replaces workers, because the one thing that a capitalist needs is the working class, and there is nothing they’d like to shed more than the working class. If they can create these stupid automatic working machines, they won’t need the working class anymore; amazing! At the very least they will have massively undermined the working class by reducing its ability to negotiate, by making the individuals more replaceable, all to capture even more value than they already are. No one knows what it means if no one on earth at that point will be able to afford anything, who cares? Workers are consumers too, what happens when workers can’t consume? ¯_(ツ)_/¯

Something that concerns me specifically is their control over the actual physical means of production. With social media we already had to contend with this; the corporations owned the servers and therefore our data. This is much worse with the so called AI guys. They own the data centres where the inference happens, we have to rent inference time from them. Like I said, I acknowledged that the things are good at some types of work, but I do not want to live in a world in which I am dependent on a third party’s goodwill to get my work done. I do not want to pay a subscription for a service that I so intimately dependent on, that can be turned off or changed without my input. This isn’t the same as paying netflix or spotify or whatever, this is paying for something that they are telling you to use to replace your brain with for your work.

You are supposed to depend on this thing; how do I do that when I have no control over it? Oh, just run a local model. Really? These assholes have made it so expensive to build a PC because they’ve destroyed the supply chain, and now they’re telling us that actually this is a good thing!

Why I’m concerned#

I’m thinking about this because I have begun to perceive a cognitive dissonance in people. Most people, I think, will agree with the premise that social media has negatively impacted society and that regulation is very badly required.

Some people do feel this way about so called AI as well, but for the most part people are very cheerfully walking right in without questioning the tool, the technology, or the corporations providing them to us. Can they do things that make your life easier? Yes. Should we just give in and let them do all the stuff then? I reject that. Social media took over our social lives. It has dramatically reduced the quality of our friendships and other relationships, and we’re not better off with it, I very strongly believe this. So called AI goes further. It is not the same, but it will accentuate and accelerate the social isolation that people feel. Why even have friends when you can chat with a robot that will always tell you you’re amazing? But beyond that, what it is made to do is to reduce your ability to participate in the economy. This is catastrophic for both, the individual and for society. What happens when people lose their ability to make money, and to contribute to society? And what happens when the economy is no longer built by people, but by machines that are given their mandate by a handful of sociopaths?

I think we need to slow down and ask questions about how to regulate these things. I’m not saying this as one of those doomers that think that the so called AI is going to download itself into a robot body and come for me like a T1000; I’m talking about this from the perspective of someone who has seen what happened to us in the 2010’s and early 2020’s. It was a slow, insidious take over of the social lives of people and we’re still struggling with the fallout. This is happening again and we need to engage with it with that level of seriousness.
